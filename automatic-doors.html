Low Energy Doors - Knowing Act

They require a conscious trigger to open, such as a push plate, wall switch, wave-to-open sensor, or wireless remote (unlike full-power sensors). Governed by the ANSI/BHMA A156.19 Standard, they take 3 to 5+ seconds to open or close and stop or reverse automatically if they hit an obstruction.

Manual Use: They still function like regular manual doors when someone prefers to push or pull them open by hand.

Full Energy Doors - Automatic

They typically use motion or presence sensors (such as microwave radars or infrared detectors) for automatic, hands-free opening without requiring a "knowing act" like a push plate. Governed by the ANSI/BHMA A156.10, they must include presence sensors to detect stationary or moving people in the swing or slide path and stop or reverse the door.

Guide Rails: Required on the swing side to prevent pedestrian collisions.

Force and Speed Limits: Maximum stopping force is capped at 40 lbf, manual opening force at 30 lbf, and breakaway force at 50 lbf.

Certification: Installation and servicing generally require certification from the American Association of Automatic Door Manufacturers (AAADM).

Universal Washroom Doors

Universal washroom automatic door systems are integrated, barrier-free access solutions designed to provide privacy, accessibility, and safety for individuals of all abilities. Under accessibility legislation 2024 BC Building Code (BCBC), these systems must follow strict sequencing rules to function safely.

How the System Works

  • Outside Activation: A push-button, wave-to-open sensor, or touchless pad opens the automatic door operator.
  • Inside Locking: Once inside, pressing a lock button, switch, or tape switch locks the door, turns on an exterior "Occupied" indicator light, and disables the outside activation button.
  • Unlocking and Exit: Pressing the internal "Open" or unlock button releases the lock and opens the door. Forced-entry or staff override features are also built-in for emergencies.
  • Emergency Systems: An internal pull-cord or emergency push button triggers audible and visual alarms both inside and outside the room.

Revolving Door Solutions

Energy-Efficient Revolving Door Design

Revolving doors lower HVAC energy costs by creating a permanent thermal seal between a building's interior and the outside environment. Unlike standard swing or sliding doors that create a direct open air path when used, revolving doors utilize an "always-open, always-closed" design. This ensures that at least one door wing constantly seals the building entrance, significantly reducing heating and cooling loads across all seasons.

Core Energy-Saving Mechanics

  • Continuous Thermal Seal: At least one wing seals the entrance at all times, preventing direct drafts and outdoor air infiltration.
  • Reduced Stack Effect: Minimizes the pressure-driven air movement common in high-rise buildings, which normally forces conditioned air out.
  • Minimized Heat Exchange: Prevents winter heat loss and summer heat gain, lowering the mechanical strain on HVAC systems.

ANSI/BHMA A156.10 (Full-Energy Automatic Doors)

Standard Overview

ANSI/BHMA A156.10 is the American National Standard for Power Operated Pedestrian Doors. It governs the safety, performance, and testing requirements for full-power automatic door systems.

Unlike low-energy doors (which are covered under A156.19 and designed for accessibility), full-energy doors operate at higher speeds and forces, requiring a robust network of safety sensors and physical barriers to protect pedestrians.

Core Requirements & Specifications

The standard establishes strict parameters for several critical areas of automatic door operation:

1. Sensor and Detection Zones

  • Motion Sensors: Actuation sensors must detect a person approaching the door opening from a specific distance, ensuring the door begins opening well before the pedestrian reaches the threshold.
  • Safety Sensors: Presence sensors must monitor the path of the door panels (both in the opening and closing cycles) to prevent the door from striking a stationary or slow-moving person.

2. Speeds and Forces

  • Opening Speed: Designed to clear the opening rapidly for high-traffic environments, though regulated to prevent violent impacts.
  • Closing Speed: Must be controlled to allow safe passage, with a minimum time limit specified based on the width and weight of the door panels.
  • Closing Force: The maximum force exerted by a closing door panel cannot exceed 30 lbf (133 N) when blocked.

3. Entrapment Prevention & Guide Rails

  • Guide Rails: For automatic swinging doors, physical guide rails (or walls) are generally required on the swing side to prevent pedestrians from inadvertently walking into the path of an opening door.
  • Safety Mats: Where electronic sensors are not used, specific pressure-sensitive mat configurations must be laid out to detect presence within the entire swing or slide zone.
  • Signage: Standardized, highly visible warning decals (e.g., "Automatic Door," "In Automatic Mode," "Do Not Enter") must be applied directly to the doors at eye level.

4. Breakout (Emergency Egress)

  • Inward/Outward Pressure: In the event of an emergency power failure or panic situation, doors must allow users to manually push them open in the direction of emergency egress (breakout mode). The force required to initiate this breakout is strictly limited.

Compliance and Inspections

To ensure ongoing safety and liability protection, facilities with A156.10 doors generally follow these maintenance practices:

  • Daily Inspections: Facility managers are encouraged to perform a simple daily safety check to verify sensor functionality and door speeds.
  • AAADM Certification: Comprehensive annual inspections and adjustments should be performed by a technician certified by the American Association of Automatic Door Manufacturers (AAADM).

ANSI/BHMA A156.19 (Power Assist and Low-Energy Power-Operated Swinging Doors)

Standard Overview

ANSI/BHMA A156.19 is the American National Standard for Power Assist and Low-Energy Power-Operated Swinging Doors. This standard specifically governs doors that are opened manually but have power assistance, or doors that open automatically upon activation but operate with strictly limited speed, force, and kinetic energy.

Unlike full-power doors (A156.10), low-energy doors are primarily designed as an accessibility aid (complying with ADA requirements) rather than for high-volume pedestrian traffic. Because they move slowly and gently, they do not require safety sensors or physical guide rails.

Core Requirements & Specifications

The standard sets strict operational limits to ensure the door cannot injure a pedestrian, even if it makes contact with them.

1. Activation & Knowing Act

  • Activation Methods: Low-energy doors must be triggered by a "knowing act". This includes pressing a wall-mounted push plate, using a remote control, or utilizing an access control swipe card.
  • Sensor Exception: They cannot be activated by automatic motion sensors that detect a person simply approaching the door (which is standard for full-energy doors).
  • Switch Location: Actuation switches must be installed within view of the door, at a height between 34 and 48 inches above the floor, and located so the user is not in the path of the swinging door.

2. Speeds and Opening Time

Because these doors lack presence sensors, their timing is highly regulated to ensure safe, predictable movement:

  • Opening Time: The door must take a minimum of 3 seconds to open from a closed position to 90 degrees.
  • Closing Time: The door must take a minimum of 3 seconds to close from 90 degrees to a point 10 degrees from full latch.
  • Hold-Open Time: Once fully open, the door must remain completely open for a minimum of 5 seconds before beginning to close.

3. Forces and Kinetic Energy

  • Opening/Closing Force: The force required to prevent a powered door from opening or closing must not exceed 15 lbf (67 N) measured at 1 inch from the latch edge of the door panel.
  • Manual Force: If the power is off or if a user chooses to operate the door manually, the force required to open the door cannot exceed 15 lbf (or 8.5 lbf for exterior doors and 5 lbf for interior doors to meet stricter ADA standards).

4. Required Signage

Standardized decals must be applied to the door at eye level (approx. 58 inches from the floor) to guide users:

  • "Knowing Act" Doors: Must feature a sign stating "Automatic Caution Door" combined with a push-plate icon or text saying "Activate Switch to Operate".
  • Power Assist Doors: Must feature a sign stating "Easy Open Door / Power Assist".

Learning Center

Quick answers to common automatic door questions.

How do automatic doors detect people?

Most use motion sensors, presence sensors, or push-button actuators. Safety sensors stop the door from closing on someone in the opening.

How often should doors be serviced?

Regular preventive maintenance is recommended. Heavy-traffic entrances generally need more frequent checks than low-use doors.

What should I do if my door fails open?

Keep people away from the doorway, secure the entrance if needed, and call for emergency service.

Sliding, swing or revolving: which fits?

Sliding suits tight, busy spaces; swing suits retrofits and accessibility upgrades; revolving suits large, climate-sensitive lobbies.

Can existing manual doors be automated?

Often yes. Many swing doors can be fitted with a low-energy operator; we’ll assess your door on site.
